# Webinar Playbook — Notes

Working notes for building **The Flow** webinar. Source: the transcript of Julian Gordon's
"Webinar Wealth Blueprint" masterclass (2 h 54 min, including a live build for a guest,
"Dr. Crystal"), read in full, plus a first review of the current Flow deck in Canva.

Companion to `7Band-Offer-Architecture.pdf`. Nothing here is legal, tax or compliance advice.

---

## 1. The framework

**Definition.** A webinar is a one-to-many sales experience where people come to know, like
and trust you quickly *because you increased their belief that they can reach their goal* —
and in exchange you get to offer more help to get there faster, safer, easier or cheaper.

**Sales** = the process of getting someone to accept help.

### The four sections

| Section | Share | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Belief reset | 40% | Answer the one question every attendee has: **"Can this work for someone like me?"** They must believe in the **message** (what), the **method** (how) and the **mentor** (who). They can borrow belief from you and from people you've helped. |
| Core teaching | 30% | Teach the **what and the why, not the how**. Teach the framework; sell the implementation. Teach enough to build confidence, not enough to do it alone. |
| Process & proof | 20% | **Show, don't tell.** Your results, client results, coaching someone live, or a quick win everyone gets right now. "Nobody can deny that you actually helped them." |
| Clear offer | 10% | The offer is the obvious next step. **Gift, not grift.** |

### The rules under it

- **Build the offer first**, then reverse-engineer the webinar from it. A PowerPoint with a
  pitch on the end is not a webinar.
- If the offer **surprises** people at the end, a step was skipped. Hesitation at checkout
  means the webinar and the offer don't match.
- **Ask permission** to make an offer before teaching: "If I help you tonight, can I take a few
  minutes at the end to offer more help to those who want it?"
- **Don't overteach.** Information overload leaves people worse off, not transformed.
- **Execution, not information.** People pay for help doing it. The offer should produce a
  win in the first 30 days.
- **Every slide moves toward one clear next step.**
- You're full-screen ~5 minutes; the slides carry the rest. A script can be read.
- **The funnel around it:** find → fill → facilitate → follow up. Reminder emails and texts
  drive attendance. Simplest version: landing page → registration → live webinar → cart or
  calendar → follow-up emails/texts.
- **Live first.** Run it live weekly, improve it, and only make it evergreen once a recording
  reliably converts.

### His offer ladder (matches the worksheets we filled)

| Rung | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| Low ticket | Brings people into the ecosystem |
| One-time offer | Shown right after webinar registration |
| **Mid ticket** | **What the webinar sells** |
| High ticket | One-on-one — usually a booked call |

Financial services and therapy-style offers often close to **book a call** instead of a cart.

## 2. What not to copy

The structure is sound and largely Russell Brunson's "Perfect Webinar" (*Expert Secrets*).
"Slidecology" and the "94 techniques" are packaging for well-known persuasion principles —
none of it requires software. Where the masterclass broke its own "gift, not grift" rule:

1. **Passing off recordings as live.** He dodged "is this actually live?" and described
   evergreen webinars that "appear live… they think it's live." **Hard no for us.** Replays are
   labelled as replays.
2. **Income claims as the main proof** ($10M, $934K in six months, $200K in five days). Our
   equivalent — score jumps, funding amounts — is exactly what CROA and the FTC prohibit.
3. **Stacked urgency.** Midnight bonus, Friday timer, "price jumps to $3,000," "first three,"
   "one more seat," "going once, going twice." Only use urgency that is literally true.
4. **Inflated value stacks** ($5,973 → "over $10,000 of true value").
5. **Agitating pain** ("lights go off"). Our audience is consumers under real financial
   stress; regulators watch credit repair marketing aimed at them most closely.

Also: his "10% offer" ran ~50 minutes of nearly three hours. Don't plan to 75 minutes off his
percentages.

## 3. Applied to The Flow

**Teach the whole map. Sell the first step.** The seven levels are the framework (the what
and why). The offer is implementation of Level 1.

| Rung | Placement |
|---|---|
| $27 Fix Your File | Registration thank-you page; downsell in follow-up emails |
| $67 Dispute Engine | Immediately after the $27 purchase |
| **$152.60/mo Restoration Program** | **The webinar close** |
| $2,997 Capital Architecture | Book-a-call, for people already past Level 1 |

**The close:** "Nothing is charged today. You pay at the end of your first month, after the
work is done." Enrollment must deliver the CROA written contract, Consumer Credit File Rights
statement and three-day cancellation window before work starts.

**Proof that fits:**
- **Live quick win:** everyone pulls their free reports at annualcreditreport.com and checks
  three things during the webinar.
- **Live demo:** a file walkthrough on a **sample or fully redacted report only** — never a
  real person's report on a public webinar.
- **Process testimonials** ("he explained every line"), not outcome testimonials.
- **Authority:** *The American Money Tree*, the insurance licence, and being the person who
  says what *can't* be removed.

**Required on slides, not in a footer:** total cost incl. IdentityIQ and the commission,
no outcome/score/timeline promises, licensed-agent and compensation disclosure, not
legal/tax advice. Any slide mentioning life insurance goes to carrier compliance first.

## 4. The Flow deck — first review (Canva "THE FLOW", pages 1–39)

The file holds two versions: a plain navy deck (pp. 1–30) and a newer game-themed
"Generational Wealth Quest" deck (pp. 31+), whose slides are flat images — the text is baked
in and can't be edited in place. Pages 40–51 not yet reviewed.

### What's working
- p. 6 — "The Flow is designed for the ones who don't have it all together." A real belief-reset line.
- p. 7 / p. 32 — the four problems (budget, credit, structure, generational plan). Clear.
- p. 16 / p. 34 — the seven-level map. This *is* the framework.
- p. 19 notes — the band-director "Get yo mind right" story and the books. The best origin
  story in the deck, currently buried in the implementation section.
- p. 35 — "Risk Audit / earning the right to take calculated risks" and p. 36 Wealth Pyramid
  ("riskier opportunities only with money you can afford to lose"). Honest and useful.

### Structural gaps
- **No defined offer.** The only CTA is "schedule a time to talk" — on p. 17, *before* the
  implementation section, so the deck keeps teaching after the ask.
- **Belief reset unwritten.** pp. 3–4 are title-only; notes are placeholders.
- **No proof.** No testimonials, demo or live win.
- **The book isn't in the deck** — the strongest authority asset is missing.
- **Overteaching / sending people elsewhere.** p. 21 notes point to Kickoff, Dovly.ai and a
  dispute-letter generator; p. 22 to bizzie.com. These hand the "how" to other companies
  before the offer — including alternatives to Arise's own service. (If any are affiliate
  links, the commission has to be disclosed.)

### Claims that contradict the book or need professional review
| Page | Claim | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| 9 notes | Automated system that "systematically improves credit scores" | Outcome claim — CROA |
| 11 notes | Access to capital "through grants" | Implies grant availability most people won't get |
| 12 notes; 14 notes | "Guaranteed Life Time Line of Credit" | Policy loans accrue interest and can lapse a policy; not a guaranteed LOC |
| 24 notes; 37 | Dynamic/velocity banking — high-limit credit cards to offset monthly expenses | Directs a credit-repair audience into revolving debt; contradicts Level 1 and the Restoration Program |
| 25, 26 | "Tax-free private banking system," "Tax-Free Loan Execution Plan" | Tax-free only while the policy stays in force and isn't a MEC — the book's own caveat |
| 38 | "Money compounds uninterrupted" | The same claim flagged on the Game Map; caps, charges and loan interest interrupt it |
| 39 | Banks hold $205B in BOLI as "tier-1 reserves… run the exact same play" | BOLI is a regulated, capped bank asset bought on institutional terms; not the same product or play |
| 13 + notes | "Shielded" asset protection — chart tops out at a **Revocable** Living Trust | A revocable trust gives no creditor protection |
| 14 notes | ILIT **and** a personal line of credit on the same policy | In an ILIT the trust owns the policy; the insured can't borrow against it personally. Contradicts Level 4 |
| 28 | Trust as beneficiary "bypasses probate entirely" | Overstated; legal territory |
| 26, 30 | Deliverables: "Irrevocable Trust Creation & Setup," "Complete Family Office Blueprint," "Business Funding Acquisition Strategy" | Trust creation is an attorney's work; funding can't be promised. Conflicts with the offer ladder |
| 3 notes | "Asset Protection Specialist" | Implies legal expertise; lead with the licence and the book instead |
