**Hard stop:** free Zoom ends the meeting at **40:00**. Start wrapping up at **28:00**. Aim to end at **30:00**.

**How to read this script.** *On screen* is what the slide shows. *Say* can be read word for word; put it
in your own voice once it's familiar. *Do* is an action. Anything in **[brackets]** is a fact only you can
fill in — don't present until every bracket is gone.

**Pace tip:** the *Say* text is about 1,700 words — roughly 13–14 minutes at a relaxed pace. The other
6–7 minutes are the chat moments and pauses. If you're running long, cut a chat moment, not the disclosures
(slides 14, 17, 18, 20).

# SECRET II · THE FOUNDATION · 7:30–11:30

## Slide 12 — What's in Your File  *(7:30)*
**On screen:** WHAT'S ACTUALLY IN YOUR FILE · Personal information · Accounts · Public records · Inquiries · Three bureaus — Equifax, Experian, TransUnion — and they don't always match

**Say:** Secret number two. Your credit report has four parts. Personal information — names, addresses, employers. Accounts — every card and loan and how you've paid. Public records, like bankruptcies. And inquiries — who's pulled your credit. There are three bureaus — Equifax, Experian and TransUnion — and they each keep their own file. They don't always match. That matters.

## Slide 13 — What You Can Challenge  *(9:00)*
**On screen:** WHAT THE LAW LETS YOU CHALLENGE · Inaccurate · Incomplete · Unverifiable · The bureau has to investigate — and fix or remove what can't be verified

**Say:** Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act you have the right to dispute information that's inaccurate, incomplete or can't be verified. The bureau has to investigate — usually within thirty days — and correct or remove what it can't verify. That's not a trick. That's the law working the way it's supposed to.

## Slide 14 — What Nobody Can Remove  *(10:15)*
**On screen:** WHAT NOBODY CAN REMOVE · Accurate negative information · It ages off on a schedule — most items 7 years, some bankruptcies 10 · What rebuilds it: on-time payments, lower balances, time

**Say:** Now the part a lot of people won't tell you. Accurate negative information — a late payment that really happened — nobody can legally remove. It ages off on a schedule: most items seven years, some bankruptcies ten. Anybody who says they can delete accurate information is lying to you. What actually rebuilds a file: paying on time, keeping balances low, and time. Not exciting. True. And true is what works.

# SECRET III · THE ENGINE · 11:30–17:00

## Slide 15 — The Wealth Pyramid  *(11:30)*
**On screen:** THE WEALTH PYRAMID · Base: Protection & certainty — guarantees, liquid cash, insurance · Middle: Growth & cash flow — real estate, business · Top: Risk & opportunity — only with money you can afford to lose

**Say:** Secret number three — the engine. Wealth is built like a pyramid. The base is protection and certainty — emergency cash, guarantees, insurance. The middle is growth and cash flow — real estate, a business. The top is risk — stocks, speculation — only with money you can afford to lose. Most people build it upside down, and one bad month knocks it over. Build from the bottom.

## Slide 16 — Level 3, Honestly  *(12:30)*
**On screen:** LEVEL 3: CAPITAL READINESS · Borrow to build assets. Never borrow to cover your bills. · A fundable business · A plan for every dollar borrowed

**Say:** Level 3 is capital — other people's money. Borrowing is a tool for building assets. It is never a way to cover your monthly bills. If you're using cards to get to the end of the month, the answer is a budget and Level 1, not more credit. Capital comes after your foundation is strong and your business is structured — so every dollar you borrow has a job.

## Slide 17 — Level 4: The Engine  *(13:30)*
**On screen:** LEVEL 4: THE ENGINE · A properly designed, overfunded permanent life policy can build cash value you can borrow against. · The truth: it has real costs · Loans accrue interest · Underfunding can lapse a policy · It's for people who actually want the insurance

**Say:** Level 4 is the engine. A permanent life insurance policy, designed and funded for cash value, can build a pool of money you can borrow against while you're alive — and it carries a death benefit for your family. Here's what most presentations leave out. It has real costs inside it. Loans accrue interest. If it's underfunded or over-borrowed, a policy can lapse — and that can cost you the coverage and create a tax bill. And it's for people who actually want the insurance, not just the account. If someone shows you this without the costs, you already have your answer.

## Slide 18 — Warning: The MEC Trap  *(15:00)*
**On screen:** WARNING: THE MEC TRAP · Fund too much, too fast in the first 7 years and the policy becomes a Modified Endowment Contract · Loans and withdrawals get taxed differently — and can be penalized · A great concept with a bad design still fails.

**Say:** One warning. Put too much money in, too fast, in the first seven years, and the policy fails the 7-pay test and becomes a Modified Endowment Contract — an MEC. Then loans and withdrawals get taxed differently, and you can owe a penalty if you're under 59½. Great concept, bad design. That's why this level isn't do-it-yourself.

## Slide 19 — Levels 5–7: The Legacy  *(16:00)*
**On screen:** THE LEGACY · 5 · The Fortress — holding company and trust, drafted by an attorney · 6 · The Transfer — beneficiaries chosen on purpose · 7 · The Generational Tree — the next generation starts ahead

**Say:** Levels five through seven are the legacy. The Fortress: a holding company and a trust, drafted by an attorney — not by me and not off the internet. A revocable living trust avoids probate for what's titled into it, but it doesn't shield you from creditors; different trusts do different jobs. The Transfer: beneficiaries chosen on purpose, not by default. And the Generational Tree: teaching your kids the map, so they start ahead instead of at Level 0. That's the whole point.
